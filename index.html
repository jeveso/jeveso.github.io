About Me

I am a PhD Candidate in the Animal–Environment Interactions Lab at the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior and Universität Konstanz, Germany. My work focuses on movement and spatial ecology, wildlife management, and species conservation, with a particular interest in birds and raptors.

Before starting my PhD, I served as a Lecturer at Federal University Gashua, Nigeria, where I taught numerous undergraduate courses, supervised research projects, and contributed to departmental leadership. I am also an active wildlife consultant, citizen scientist, and conservation advocate.

Teaching & Mentorship

I have taught and assessed a wide range of undergraduate courses in agriculture, forestry, wildlife ecology, and conservation science, and have supervised multiple undergraduate research projects.

Projects

Vulture Movement & Global Change

Spatio-temporal analysis of vulture behavior and space use in response to global change, presented at IMPRS-QBEE retreats and symposia.

Drylands Project (MPI-AB)

Global dataset analysis on vulture movement ecology, including meta-information stratification and ecological modeling.

Avifauna Diversity Studies in Nigeria

Field studies and publications on bird diversity, abundance, and ecological distribution across Nigerian ecosystems.

Wildlife Consultancy & Zoo Management

Enclosure design, enrichment, taxidermy, park landscaping, and conservation education across multiple wildlife facilities.
